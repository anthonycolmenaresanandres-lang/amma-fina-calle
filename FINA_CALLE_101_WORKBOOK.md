## Lesson 1 — What Fina Calle Sells & Why Owners Buy

**Objective:** Be able to explain, in one breath, what Fina Calle gives a café and why an owner would happily pay for it.

### The Idea

Fina Calle is a small agency. In plain terms, an agency is a team you hire to do something for you. What Fina Calle does is take a café or restaurant's ordinary paper menu and turn it into a **branded digital experience** — something fun and modern that lives on a phone.

"Digital experience" sounds fancy, but here's all it means: a customer at the café scans a little square barcode (a **QR code** — the kind you point your phone camera at) and instantly sees the menu on their screen. And it doesn't stop at the menu — there's also a little game with the café's name and look on it, so customers stick around and have fun while they wait.

Why do owners buy this? Because it makes their café feel modern and memorable, it keeps customers engaged at the table, and it costs them far less than hiring a tech team. They get something that looks like a big brand made it, without the big-brand price. You're not selling "technology" — you're selling **a café that feels alive and modern.**

The most powerful part of your job is that you don't have to convince anyone with a speech. You just show them something real that already exists.

### Colattao Example

Colattao is a real café, and it's the proof. Its current live link is **colattao-cafe-rush.vercel.app**, with a **menu** page at the same link followed by **/menu**. You scan the QR code (or open the link), the Colattao menu appears, and there's a button to play their branded game — a quick **soccer penalty-shootout game called "Penalty Rush"** (pick your corner, beat the goalkeeper, try to score 3 out of 5).

When you show a café owner Colattao on your phone, you're not describing an idea — you're handing them a finished, working example of exactly what they could have. That's the entire pitch, and it's why Colattao matters so much.

> **One habit that saves you:** open the demo yourself before any pitch trip and notice **exactly what the game is and what it's called** — because the name on the button is what you'll say out loud. Product names can change, so **confirm the current live link with Anthony before each trip and bookmark whatever he gives you.** Think of it as "the current live Colattao link," not a fixed address you memorize.

## Lesson 2 — The Offer: The 4 Deliverables and the 4 Tiers

**Objective:** Name the four things Fina Calle can deliver, and put the four pricing tiers in the right order.

### The Idea

A "deliverable" is simply a finished thing the client gets. Fina Calle has **four deliverables**:

1. **A digital menu** you reach by scanning a QR code.
2. **A branded mini-game** (a small, fun game made over in the café's name and look).
3. **Optionally, a real website.**
4. **A short launch video** to announce it.

Clients pay in a simple way: a **one-time setup fee** (paid once, to build it) plus a **monthly fee** (paid every month, to keep it running and supported).

Those deliverables stack into **four tiers** — think of it as a ladder, where each step up adds something to the step below. **You don't memorize dollar amounts. Anthony confirms the final price on every deal.** Your job is just to know the *order* of the ladder and what each step adds:

- **Menú** — the QR digital menu, plus a simple **dashboard** (a screen the owner uses to edit their own menu).
- **Juego** — everything in Menú, **plus** a branded mini-game.
- **Marca** — everything in Juego, **plus** a real website.
- **OS** — everything in Marca, **plus** seasonal game and campaign refreshes (fresh updates each season), on an ongoing monthly arrangement.

> **What can you actually sell right now?** Today you can sell **Juego and up.** The **Menú** entry offer isn't live yet — it rolls out after we measure how Colattao's launch performs. So if an owner only wants the menu, just say: *"Let me check with Anthony and get right back to you."*

A couple of things worth knowing so you're never confused on a call:

**The founding deal.** Colattao, our first client, is locked at a special low price **forever.** That was on purpose — the low price was the cost of proving the business works. **New clients are not quoted that low.** Colattao is special; don't use its price with anyone else.

**The founding café offer** (a handy sales tool). The first **2–3 cafés in a new city** get a locked discount, in exchange for giving a **testimonial** (a short quote you can show other owners) and being a **local reference** (an owner who'll vouch for you if asked). It's a friendly "you're helping us get started here, so here's a deal" arrangement.

> **Reference box — Anthony's eyes, not yours to memorize.** You never have to recite these. They're here only so the numbers don't surprise you if Anthony mentions them.
> - **Menú** entry offer (not live yet): around **$100 setup, first month free**.
> - **Juego**: roughly **$1,500–2,000 setup / $250–350 a month**.
> - **Marca** and **OS**: priced by quote (Anthony gives a custom price).
> - **Colattao founding deal**: **$900 setup / $150 a month, forever** — Colattao only.
> - The exact currency (US dollars vs. Colombian pesos) is still being finalized — another reason you don't memorize amounts.

### Colattao Example

Colattao has the menu *and* the game ("Penalty Rush"), so it lives at the **Juego** level of the ladder. But its price is the special founding deal — not the standard Juego range. This is the perfect example of the rule: the *tier* tells you what's included; *Anthony* tells you the final price.

## Lesson 3 — The Operator's 4 Jobs (The Heart of It)

**Objective:** Know your four jobs cold — what each one is, and the fact that everything *else* belongs to the agents.

### The Idea

This is the most important lesson, so take your time. Everything you do as the Operator fits into **four jobs.** If you remember nothing else from this whole workbook, remember these four words: **Close, Collect, Approve, Bill.**

**Job 1 — CLOSE.** Get a café to say yes. You do this by showing them Colattao on your phone. You're not giving a hard sales pitch — you're showing a real, working example and letting it speak. "We built this for them. Yours in days." Then you help them pick a tier and you get a yes **and a deposit** — *a deposit is a part of the setup fee paid up front to start the work. Anthony tells you the exact amount for that deal; you just collect it* (it ties right back to the "Anthony confirms the price" rule).

**Job 2 — COLLECT.** Once they say yes, gather a few simple things from the owner (you'll learn the exact list in Lesson 4). Pictures, their menu, their business info. You put it all in one shared folder and hand it to an agent. That's the collecting.

**Job 3 — APPROVE.** The agent builds everything and sends you a preview link. You open it on your phone and look it over against a short checklist (right logo? prices match what the owner gave you? photos correct? does the game open and play? hours and Instagram right?). If it all looks right, you say **"ship it."** If something's off, you say exactly what to fix and send it back. You're the quality check — the human eye that says yes or no.

**Job 4 — BILL.** Once it's live, you start the monthly charge. Here's how that actually works at your level: **Anthony sets up the payment method with you in advance** (a simple invoice link the café pays, or a recurring charge he wires up that you just switch on). When the café goes live, **you send the charge and confirm the money arrived.** The amount always comes from Anthony — you're never guessing a number. *Your real job here is knowing **when** to start it: the day it goes live.* Anthony will walk you through the exact button in person, so this part is never as scary as it sounds.

Here's the freeing part: **everything that isn't one of these four jobs belongs to the agents.** You do not build the menu, you do not make the game, you do not edit video, you do not write code. A simple rule of thumb to keep you out of the weeds:

> **If a task isn't a sales chat, a photo, an approval, or money — hand it to an agent.**

The four jobs are all very human: talking to people, collecting real things, judging quality, and handling money. Those are things only you can do. The rest is the agents' job.

### Colattao Example

Colattao went through all four jobs. Someone **closed** it (showed the idea, got the yes and a deposit). Someone **collected** Colattao's logo, menu, and photos. Someone **approved** the finished menu and the "Penalty Rush" game — looked at it and said "ship it." And someone started the **bill** — the monthly charge. Today Colattao is live and is the proof you use to close the *next* café. The loop pays off.

## Lesson 4 — The Client Loop: From "Yes" to "Launched" in 5 Steps

**Objective:** Walk a café through the full journey, in order, from first hello to live-and-billing.

### The Idea

The four jobs from Lesson 3 happen inside a simple, repeatable journey. Think of it as a path with a beginning and an end. There's one thing you do *once and keep forever*, and then five steps you run for every new café.

**Step 0 (once, forever): Keep the live Colattao demo on your phone.** That's your whole pitch, always ready. Confirm the current link with Anthony and bookmark it. Don't skip this — it's the tool you carry everywhere.

**Step 1 — CLOSE.** Walk into a café or send a message. Show them Colattao's menu and game on your phone: *"We built this for them — yours in days."* Help them pick a tier. Get a **yes** and the **deposit** (the up-front part of the setup fee — Anthony tells you the amount).

**Step 2 — COLLECT.** Get these from the owner and put them all in **one shared folder** (a Google Drive folder Anthony sets up with you — you drop everything into it, then copy its "share link"):
- Their **logo** (an image file).
- Their **menu** (items + prices — a clear photo or typed out, either is fine).
- **3–5 photos** of the place and their signature product.
- **Owner photos** (optional — only if they want to be on camera).
- **Business info** (name, hours, phone, Instagram, any links).

Then **hand it to an agent** — which simply means: open the agent's chat, paste in your handoff sentence (Lesson 5), and include the folder's share link. That's the whole "handoff."

**Step 3 — BUILD (the agent does this; you wait).** The agent makes the menu page, **gives the game the café's look (its colors and logo),** makes the launch video, generates the QR code, and puts it all online. **You do nothing in this step** — that's by design. Rest easy here.

**Step 4 — APPROVE.** The agent sends you a **preview link** (a private web link to look at the work before it's public). Open it on your phone and run the quick checklist:
- Right **logo** and café name?
- Do the **prices** match what the owner gave you?
- Are the **photos** the right ones?
- Does the **game** open and play?
- Are the **hours, phone, and Instagram** correct?

If yes to all, reply **"ship it."** If not, reply with exactly what to fix and send it back. **Also ask the agent for the QR code image** (they generate it) so you can get it printed before launch.

**Step 5 — LAUNCH & BILL.** Put the **QR sticker** up at the café — the agent gives you the QR code as an image; you (or Anthony) get it printed as a sticker or order one, and he'll show you where. **Post the launch video** on the café's own Instagram (the owner gives you access), or simply hand the video to the owner to post. Then **start the monthly charge** (the billing step from Lesson 3 — the day it goes live is your cue). Done — and that café is now a reference you can show to win the next one.

That last part is the quiet magic: every café you launch becomes proof for the next café. The loop feeds itself.

### Colattao Example

Run the steps against Colattao and they all line up: it was closed (yes + deposit), its materials were collected into a folder, the agents built the menu and "Penalty Rush," it was approved, then it launched with a printed QR sticker and went onto its monthly billing. Now Colattao *is* your Step 0 demo — the finished client you show at the start of every new pitch.

## Lesson 5 — Working With the Agents + Your One Handoff Sentence

**Objective:** Confidently hand a whole build to an agent using a single sentence — and know exactly where you type it.

### The Idea

You've seen that the agents do all the building. So how do you actually *use* them? You open the agent's chat (the text box Anthony sets up with you on Day 1), start a new message, and **type one sentence.** No special skill, no technical words.

Here's the whole secret. After you've closed a café and collected their stuff into your shared folder, you paste this in:

> **"New client: [café name], [tier]. Their logo / menu / photos / links are in this folder: [paste the share link]. Build the digital menu + a version of the game in their colors and logo + a launch video, and send me a preview link."**

That's it. That one sentence kicks off the entire build. You fill in the café's name, the tier they picked, and the folder's share link — and the agent takes it from there. **That sentence is the skill.** Master it and you've mastered working with the agents.

Notice what the sentence does. It tells the agent *who* (the café), *what level* (the tier), *where the materials are* (the folder link), and *what you want back* (a preview link). It maps perfectly onto your jobs: you've already Closed and Collected, this sentence starts the Build, and the preview link sets up your Approve.

### What a real round-trip looks like

So you can picture the whole back-and-forth, here's one full exchange — all in the **same chat window**:

1. **You type:** *"New client: Café Luna, Juego. Their logo / menu / photos / links are in this folder: [share link]. Build the digital menu + a version of the game in their colors and logo + a launch video, and send me a preview link."*
2. **The agent replies** (a little later) with something like: *"Done — here's your preview: [a link]. The QR code image is attached too."* You tap that link to see the work.
3. **You review it** on your phone against the Approve checklist from Lesson 4.
4. **You type back, in the same chat:** *"Ship it."* — or — *"Almost. Two fixes: the logo is blurry, and the price for the latte should be $5, not $4. Then re-send the preview."*

That's the entire relationship: one sentence out, a preview back, you reply "ship it" or list the fixes. No magic, no jargon.

### Who does what (keep yourself in your lane)

- **Only you can do:** the sales conversation, taking and collecting real photos, saying yes or no (the approval), and collecting money.
- **The agents do:** build the menu page, give the game the café's look, make and export the video, generate the QR code, write the copy, and **deploy** (put it online).

If you ever feel unsure whether something is yours, go back to the rule of thumb: *if it isn't a sales chat, a photo, an approval, or money, hand it to an agent.*

## When You're Not Sure What to Say (Your Safety Net)

You will hit moments that aren't in any script — that's normal, and you're never trapped. Your all-purpose safe answer, any time you're unsure, is:

> **"Great question — let me check with Anthony and get right back to you."**

That's not a weakness; it's the professional move. A few common moments:

- **"Your price is too high."** → *"I hear you. Let me talk to Anthony about what works for your café and come back with options."* (Price is always Anthony's call — never improvise a discount.)
- **The preview has something off and you can't describe the fix.** → Just say what you *see*, plainly: *"The logo looks blurry"* or *"This price is wrong — it should be $5."* The agent figures out the technical part. You don't need the right words, only the honest observation.
- **A customer can't get the QR code to work.** → Have them open their phone camera and point it at the code, or type the link in by hand. If it still won't open, tell Anthony — it's a setup thing, not your fault.

Whenever in doubt: pause, don't guess, check with Anthony. That single habit will carry you through your first dozen cafés.

## Words in Plain English (Glossary)

*Whenever a word trips you up, it's here — no need to hunt back through the lessons.*

- **Agent (Claude / Codex)** — an AI assistant you message in a chat box; it does the building. You type a request in plain English; it does the work.
- **Agency** — a team you hire to do something for you. Fina Calle is one.
- **Dashboard** — a simple screen where the café owner can edit their own menu.
- **Deliverable** — a finished thing the client gets (the menu, the game, the website, the video).
- **Deploy** — put the finished work online so the public can see it. The agents do this.
- **Deposit** — a part of the setup fee paid up front to start the work. Anthony sets the amount.
- **Local reference** — an owner who'll vouch for you to other owners if asked.
- **Preview link** — a private web link to look at the work before it goes public, so you can approve it.
- **QR code** — the square barcode you point a phone camera at; scanning it opens the menu.
- **Setup fee / monthly fee** — paid once to build it; paid every month to keep it running and supported.
- **Tier** — a step on the price ladder (Menú → Juego → Marca → OS); each adds something to the one below.
- **Testimonial** — a short quote from a happy owner you can show to other owners.
