# Ethical behavioral design

Use these principles to make a decision easier to understand and act on. Do not use them to conceal facts or pressure someone past informed choice.

## Applied principles

1. **Self-recognition:** lead with the owner's observed workflow or stated priority, not AMMA's feature inventory.
2. **Processing fluency:** use plain language, a visible hierarchy, and one message per section. Easier processing can improve positive judgments, so clarity is part of persuasion, not decoration.
3. **Limited choice:** present one recommended pilot and at most one alternative. Large assortments can reduce action; do not create artificial restrictions.
4. **Relevant social proof:** use Colattao only for what is verified. Never imply broad adoption or typical results from one client.
5. **Loss framing:** describe the observable cost of the current workflow, such as stale information or vendor waiting, without exaggerating fear or inventing revenue loss.
6. **Endowment through tailoring:** let the owner see a clearly labeled mock using approved brand inputs. The owner must know it is a concept, not a finished or public product.
7. **Risk reduction:** use transparent scope, exact price, what is not included, a reversible pilot, and a clear support path.
8. **Implementation intention:** close with a specific when/where next action instead of a vague intention to reconnect.
9. **Autonomy:** make no, later, and questions easy. Consent and cancellation must be as clear as acceptance.

## Prohibited dark patterns

- fake urgency, countdowns, demand, scarcity, testimonials, activity, or endorsements;
- hidden fees, recurring terms, exclusions, or cancellation paths;
- preselected consent, privacy steering, unnecessary data collection, or confusing options;
- shame, confirm-shaming, obstruction, bait-and-switch, disguised advertising, or repeated nagging;
- exploiting protected traits, children, illness, financial distress, or cognitive vulnerability;
- stating engagement, retention, traffic, revenue, or sales results without account-native evidence.

- Reber, Winkielman, and Schwarz, [processing fluency experiments](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-9280.00008).
- Iyengar and Lepper, [choice-overload experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11138768/).

- Kahneman and Tversky, [prospect theory](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i332789).

These studies support testable design hypotheses, not guaranteed sales outcomes for AMMA's customers.
