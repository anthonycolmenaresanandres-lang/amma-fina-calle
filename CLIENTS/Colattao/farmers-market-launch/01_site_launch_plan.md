# Site-Specific Launch Plan

Market name: Colattao Community Market  
Property: Colattao Coffee House, 1115 Independence Blvd Suite 101/102, Virginia Beach, VA 23455  
Pilot: 8-12 vendors, Saturday, 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM  
Status: Draft. Confirm requirements with property manager, city/zoning, fire prevention, and health department before public announcement.

## Event Concept

Colattao Community Market is a small neighborhood bazaar anchored by Colattao Coffee House. The first event should be a controlled arts-and-crafts pilot that proves the property can host a low-impact, community-first market without hurting parking, traffic, tenant operations, emergency access, ADA access, or food-safety compliance.

The pilot should feel local, clean, organized, and useful: crafts, art, flowers, makers, packaged goods, family-friendly sellers, and low-conflict neighborhood vendors. Cooked/prepared food should come from approved inside tenants or separately approved food trucks, not ordinary yellow-patch table vendors.

Long-term vision: use this bazaar as the first proof point for a larger coffee fest / coffee expo that could eventually bring regional coffee shops together at a convention-center scale.

## Yellow Patch Use

Use the highlighted yellow parking-space patch near Colattao as the proposed closed vendor-table bazaar zone, subject to approval.

Approved yellow-zone uses to request:

- Colattao table
- Vendor check-in
- QR vendor directory
- Sponsor banner
- Market information
- Customer questions and lost/found
- Printed site map and vendor list
- 8-12 small arts/crafts/maker tables if the area can be safely closed
- Clear pedestrian path around and through the zone

The yellow patch can be the main vendor table area, but only if closing those parking spaces does not block building access, sidewalks, tenant frontage, accessible route, emergency access, fire lane, drive aisle, or required customer parking.

## Layout Assumptions

- Vendor booths should sit inside the approved yellow parking-space closure zone first, with overflow only along approved parking edges or other landlord/fire-approved areas.
- Vendor rows should not cross drive aisles.
- Vendor tables should not block tenant doors, windows, ramps, sidewalks, accessible parking, or emergency lanes.
- Customer walking paths should be clear, obvious, and at least wide enough for normal accessible movement.
- Ordinary table vendors should focus on arts, crafts, makers, flowers, gifts, packaged goods, and low-conflict products.
- Cooked/prepared food should come from approved tenants inside or separately approved food trucks only.
- Any food truck, generator, cooking, propane, tent, canopy, or open-flame use requires extra review with landlord, fire, city/zoning, and health.
- No full-property activation until the pilot proves operations.

## Parking And Traffic Plan

Pilot goal: protect normal tenant operations.

- Count total spaces during site walk.
- Mark the yellow parking-space closure zone separately from customer parking.
- Mark any overflow vendor edges separately from customer spaces.
- Preserve accessible spaces and accessible route.
- Keep drive aisles open from every entrance to every exit.
- Create a vendor loading window before customer arrival, recommended 7:30 AM to 8:30 AM.
- Create vendor load-out after market close, recommended 1:15 PM to 2:00 PM.
- Do not allow vendor vehicles to idle in front of tenant entrances.
- Place "Market Info / QR Directory" at the yellow bazaar zone entrance so customers do not wander into restricted lanes.

## Fire Lane And Emergency Access Assumptions

- Fire lanes and emergency access remain open at all times.
- Drive aisles remain wide enough for emergency vehicles.
- No booth, tent, banner, trash bin, sandwich board, or line queue may reduce required emergency access.
- Any cooking, fuel, generator, extension cord, canopy, tent, or temporary electrical use must be disclosed to Fire Prevention.
- The site map must be reviewed before approval.

## ADA And Accessibility Checklist

- No booths in accessible parking spaces.
- No blocked ramps.
- No blocked sidewalks.
- No blocked tenant doors.
- Maintain clear walking path from customer parking to and through the yellow bazaar zone.
- Keep cords, mats, signs, and tent weights out of the main walking path.
- Ensure QR directory has a simple printed vendor list backup for customers who cannot scan.

## Restroom, Trash, And Security Plan

Restrooms:

- Confirm whether Colattao restrooms can be used by market customers and vendors.
- If not, ask the city/health department whether portable restroom service is required for the pilot.
- Post restroom access rules at the yellow bazaar zone only after landlord/tenant approval.

Trash:

- Place trash bags or bins at the yellow bazaar zone and any approved overflow edge.
- Require every vendor to remove their own booth trash.
- Assign a market closer to sweep the lot after shutdown.
- Do not use tenant dumpsters unless landlord approves.

Security:

- Assign one market lead and one floating helper.
- Keep a printed emergency contact sheet at the hub.
- For the first pilot, avoid alcohol, amplified music, late hours, and high-risk activities.
- If police/security review recommends paid detail or off-duty support, confirm before approval.

## Vendor Category Strategy

Protect existing tenants. Do not recruit vendors that directly compete with Nara Sushi, Subway, or Colattao unless those businesses explicitly approve.

Priority categories:

- Flowers
- Crafts
- Family-friendly makers
- Plants
- Local art
- Candles, soaps, gifts
- Community groups
- Packaged goods
- Non-prepared specialty products
- Coffee culture vendors that do not sell competing prepared drinks unless Colattao approves

Avoid or pre-approve:

- Sushi, subs, full coffee service, espresso bars, or direct meal competitors
- Cooked/prepared-food table vendors in the yellow patch
- Loud demos
- High-smoke cooking
- Vendors needing major power
- Products that create trash, odor, smoke, or crowding issues

## Approval Sequence

1. Property manager/landlord permission to explore the pilot and conduct a site walk.
2. Site walk with photos, parking count, entrances, drive aisles, yellow parking-space closure zone, overflow vendor edges, trash/restroom access, and emergency lane marked.
3. Neighbor tenant outreach and support/no-objection tracking.
4. City Outdoor Event / Special Events call to confirm whether a permit is required for this private-property pilot.
5. Zoning call to confirm private property outdoor activity and any temporary/vendor limitations.
6. Fire Prevention call to review site map, tents/canopies, generators, cooking, fire lane, emergency access, and inspection needs.
7. Virginia Beach Health Department Environmental Health call to confirm temporary food vendor path.
8. Insurance confirmation with landlord requirements.
9. Final landlord approval of site map, date, vendor count, operating rules, and proof of city/fire/health path.
10. Public date announcement only after the above is confirmed.

## Rain Date And Cancellation Policy

- Select a rain date before public announcement.
- Recommended rain date: next available Saturday, 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM, subject to property approval.
- Cancel or postpone for severe weather, unsafe wind for tents, lightning risk, flooding, or city/property/fire direction.
- Market lead makes weather call by 5:00 PM the day before when forecast is clearly unsafe, or by 6:30 AM day-of for sudden weather.
- Vendors receive email/text notice and QR directory update.
- Booth fees can roll to the rain date for the pilot. Refunds should be case-by-case until a formal vendor agreement is approved.

## Launch Timeline

- Week 1: landlord packet, permission to site walk, tenant outreach begins.
- Week 2: city/zoning/fire/health calls, draft site map, approval evidence log.
- Week 3: vendor recruitment, vendor approvals, sponsor ask, insurance confirmation.
- Week 4: final site map, reminders, QR vendor directory, day-of staffing plan.
- Event day: 7:30 AM vendor load-in, 8:45 AM hub ready, 9:00 AM market open, 1:00 PM close, 2:00 PM site cleared.
