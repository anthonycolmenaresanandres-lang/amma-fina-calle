# Colattao Community Market - Landlord Approval Packet

Prepared for: Property manager / landlord for 1115 Independence Blvd Suite 101/102, Virginia Beach, VA 23455  
Prepared by: Anthony / AMMA Ventures LLC DBA Fina Calle, in support of Colattao Coffee House  
Concept: Colattao Community Market, a small arts-and-crafts bazaar pilot  
Proposed first event: Small Saturday pilot, 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM, 8-12 vendors

## The Ask

Colattao Coffee House would like permission to explore a small, controlled community bazaar pilot at the shopping center. This is not a full-property takeover. The revised intent is to use the highlighted yellow parking-space patch near Colattao as the closed-off vendor table zone for arts, crafts, makers, flowers, packaged goods, and family-friendly vendors, subject to landlord, fire, city/zoning, ADA/accessibility, and traffic approval.

Prepared or cooked food should not be sold from ordinary table vendors in the yellow patch. Customers who want cooked food can come inside to approved tenants, and any food truck or outside prepared-food vendor would need separate landlord, city/zoning, fire, and health department approval.

Before any public event date is announced, Anthony will confirm the required property, zoning/city, fire, and health department path.

## Why This Helps The Property

- Brings neighborhood foot traffic to the center during a limited, predictable morning window.
- Positions the center as a local gathering point without blocking normal tenant operations.
- Gives nearby tenants a chance to receive new customers before and after the market.
- Starts with a small pilot so the landlord can evaluate real impact before approving repeat dates.
- Uses Fina Calle OS to manage vendor applications, approvals, QR directory, check-in, reminders, and reporting.

## Proposed Site Use

The yellow patch near Colattao should be reviewed as the closed vendor-table bazaar zone:

- Colattao table
- Vendor check-in
- QR vendor directory
- Sponsor banner
- Market information
- Customer questions
- 8-12 small vendor tables for arts/crafts/makers if the space can be safely closed off
- Clear customer walking path around the closed area

The yellow patch may become the main vendor table area, but only if the property manager, city/zoning, fire, and accessibility review confirm the parking spaces can be temporarily closed without blocking drive aisles, fire lanes, tenant entrances, accessible parking, sidewalks, ramps, emergency access, or required customer parking.

## Safety And Access Controls

The pilot will be designed around these non-negotiables:

- Keep drive aisles clear.
- Keep fire lanes and emergency access clear.
- Keep tenant entrances clear.
- Do not use accessible parking spaces for booths.
- Do not block ramps, sidewalks, or required accessible paths.
- Keep customer parking available.
- Use controlled vendor loading and unloading windows.
- Keep cooked/prepared food sales inside approved tenants or in separately approved food trucks only.
- Provide trash plan, basic incident contact, and day-of market lead.

## Neighbor Tenant Support Plan

Anthony will seek support or no-objection feedback from:

- US Air Force Recruiting
- Army Recruiting
- Nara Sushi
- Hair By the C
- Dave's Toys and Collectibles
- Paris Nails Spa

The vendor category plan is designed to protect existing tenants. The market should avoid direct conflict with Nara Sushi, Subway, and Colattao unless those tenants approve it. Priority vendor categories are arts, crafts, family-friendly makers, flowers, plants, packaged goods, gifts, local artisans, and low-conflict specialty goods. This pilot can become the proof-of-concept for a larger future coffee fest or coffee expo that brings regional coffee shops together, potentially at a convention-center scale after the local bazaar model is proven.

## Pilot Format

- Date: to be confirmed only after approvals.
- Time: Saturday, 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM.
- Vendor count: 8-12 for first pilot.
- Future expansion: 20 vendors only after the pilot proves parking, traffic, access, tenant support, and city/fire/health path are workable.
- Long-term vision: use the bazaar pilot as a stepping stone toward a larger coffee fest / coffee expo with regional coffee shops, ideally moving to a convention-center venue when the concept is proven.
- Music: no amplified music unless city/property approval confirms it.
- Food: cooked/prepared food stays inside approved tenants or separately approved food trucks. The yellow patch should focus on arts, crafts, makers, flowers, and low-conflict bazaar vendors.
