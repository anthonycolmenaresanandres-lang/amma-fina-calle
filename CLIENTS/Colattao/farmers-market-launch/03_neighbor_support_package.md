We are not announcing a public date yet. First we are asking the landlord, nearby tenants, city/zoning, fire prevention, and health department what would be required.

The yellow parking-space area near Colattao would be the proposed closed-off vendor table zone for arts, crafts, flowers, makers, packaged goods, and family-friendly vendors. Cooked food would stay with approved inside tenants or separately approved food trucks, so the yellow table zone does not become an uncontrolled food court.

Would your business be open to supporting the idea, or at least giving no-objection feedback if the final layout protects your entrance, parking, deliveries, and customers?

## Neighbor FAQ

### Will this block my entrance?

No. Tenant entrances are protected. The site walk will mark every entrance, and the final layout should keep all doors, sidewalks, ramps, and storefront access clear.

### Will this take all the parking?

No. The pilot is intentionally small. It may temporarily close the yellow parking-space patch for vendor tables, but customer parking must remain available and the closure must be approved before announcement.

### Will food vendors compete with existing tenants?

The vendor strategy is to protect existing tenants. The yellow patch should focus on arts, crafts, makers, flowers, packaged goods, and gifts. Cooked/prepared food should be sold by approved inside tenants or separately approved food trucks only. The market should avoid direct competition with Nara Sushi, Subway, and Colattao unless those tenants approve.

### Will there be music or noise?

The pilot should avoid amplified music unless the property manager and city path confirm it is allowed. The market should be morning-focused and low-noise.

### Who handles trash?

Market operations should include trash bags/bins at the hub, vendor pack-out rules, and a final site sweep. Tenant dumpsters should not be used unless the property manager approves.

### What happens if it rains?

The market should use a rain date or cancellation policy. Vendors should receive notice by the prior evening when possible, or early day-of for sudden weather.

## Objection Handling

Parking complaints:

- Start with a small 8-12 vendor pilot.
- Keep vendor vehicles in approved vendor parking/loading areas.
- Count spaces before final layout.
- Preserve accessible parking and tenant customer parking.

Traffic flow:

- Keep drive aisles open.
- Stagger vendor load-in before the market opens.
- Do not allow vendor vehicles to idle in front of storefronts.

Trash:

- Require vendor pack-out.
- Place bins at the hub.
- Assign a closing sweep.
- Do not use tenant dumpsters without approval.

Noise:

- No amplified music unless approved.
- Keep operating hours to 9 AM to 1 PM.
- Keep entertainment family-friendly and low-volume.

Safety:

- Keep emergency access open.
- Keep fire lanes clear.
- Confirm layout with landlord and fire/city reviewers.

Competition with tenants:

- Avoid direct category conflicts.
- Invite tenant cross-promotion.
- Feature tenant offers in the QR directory when approved.
- Make the bazaar a traffic driver for tenants, not a substitute for their food/service sales.

## Tenant-Specific Notes

- Nara Sushi: avoid sushi/Japanese meal vendors unless approved.
- Subway: avoid sandwich/sub vendors unless approved.
- Colattao: avoid independent coffee/espresso competitors unless approved. Long-term coffee fest/expo invitations should be handled as a separate larger concept, not the first parking-lot bazaar pilot.
- Recruiting offices: protect walk-in access, visibility, and any reserved/required spaces.
- Personal service tenants: protect appointment parking and entrance access.
