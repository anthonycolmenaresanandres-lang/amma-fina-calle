## Friction worth showing, not insulting

1. The guest journey is split across MenuChow, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, a 14-page PDF, social profiles, and the main website.
2. The official website's Grubhub button resolves to the Princess Anne location at 4540 Princess Anne Road rather than Lynnhaven at 1009 Lynnhaven Mall Loop.
3. Public hours vary by channel: the official site lists Mon-Sat 11:00 a.m.-10:00 p.m. and Sunday 11:00 a.m.-9:00 p.m.; marketplace pages show different ordering cutoffs.
4. The table experience repeatedly appears in reviews - fast chips and salsa, tableside guacamole, big margaritas, large portions, and busy Friday nights - but none of those signature moments has a table-side digital layer.
5. The restaurant has a large menu. A permanent QR can reduce menu-search friction without forcing guests to order from their phones.

## Objection handling

**"We already have MenuChow."**  
Good - this is not a MenuChow replacement. MenuChow handles direct online ordering and loyalty; Fina Calle starts at the physical table and can hand off to the systems you keep.

**"We do not want another tablet."**  
Then the pilot should not require one. First confirm whether requests can use the current printer, POS, SMS, or a manager device.

**"Guests do not want QR ordering."**  
Ordering can stay optional. Start with menu lookup, service requests, and the table game while servers continue to own the order.

**"Staff will get spammed."**  
Production adds acknowledgment, cooldowns, duplicate suppression, assigned-server routing, and manager escalation. The demo sends nothing.

**"We are too busy."**  
That is the use case: fewer repeated walk-bys for chips, checks, and status questions during the peak.

## Activation gate

Do not call the build operational until all of these are confirmed:

- written owner approval for the guest-facing concept;
- exact table count and labels;
- owner-confirmed menu, prices, modifiers, and availability;
- in-house POS and hardware;
- staff recipient and escalation workflow;
- approved logo, colors, and imagery if they want client branding;
- whether any provider supports verified per-table dine-in checkout;
- two-phone game QA and scans of every final QR on iOS and Android.
